HYPOTHESIS 1: SOCS3-MICA/MICB Immune Evasion Axis in COVID-19
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('SOCS3',negative_correlation,'MICA')   [line 659]
  event('SOCS3',negative_correlation,'MICB')   [line 651]
  event('SOCS3',positive_correlation,'IFNG')   [line 653]
  event('SOCS3',positive_correlation,'TNF')    [line 661]
  event('SOCS3',positive_correlation,'STAT3')  [line 656]

Mechanism:
  MICA and MICB are stress-induced ligands for NKG2D (KLRK1), a critical
  activating receptor on NK cells and CD8+ T cells. SOCS3 (Suppressor of
  Cytokine Signaling 3) negatively regulates MICA/MICB expression. In the
  context of COVID-19, elevated SOCS3 -- driven by the hyperinflammatory
  cytokine storm (IFNG, TNF, STAT3) -- may suppress NKG2D ligand expression,
  thereby dampening NK and CD8+ T cell-mediated clearance of SARS-CoV-2-
  infected cells.

Prediction:
  COVID-19 patients with elevated SOCS3 would show:
  (a) Reduced MICA/MICB surface expression on infected epithelial cells
  (b) Impaired NKG2D-mediated NK cell cytotoxicity
  (c) More severe disease outcomes

Testable via: SOCS3 and MICA/MICB expression in COVID-19 patient PBMCs,
              NK cell degranulation assays (CD107a/LAMP1 upregulation).

Novelty: SOCS3 as an immune evasion mechanism is known in cancer and
         hepatitis, but unexplored in SARS-CoV-2 infection.

HYPOTHESIS 2: EZH2 as a Driver of COVID-19 CD8+ Lymphopenia
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('EZH2',negative_correlation,'CD8A')    [line 985]
  event('EZH2',negative_correlation,'GZMB')    [line 978]
  event('EZH2',association,'B2M')              [line 987]
  event('EZH2',association,'TAP1')             [line 989]
  event('CD8A',negative_correlation,'EZH2')    [line 986]

Mechanism:
  EZH2 is the catalytic subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2),
  which deposits H3K27me3 marks to silence gene expression. EZH2-mediated
  silencing of CD8A and GZMB (Granzyme B) suggests a direct epigenetic
  mechanism for CD8+ T cell dysfunction. COVID-19 is characterized by
  profound CD8+ lymphopenia, especially in severe cases. EZH2 also
  paradoxically associates with B2M and TAP1 (MHC-I components), indicating
  a complex, context-dependent regulation of antigen presentation.

Prediction:
  Pharmacological EZH2 inhibition (e.g., with GSK-2816126 or tazemetostat)
  would restore CD8+ T cell numbers and granzyme B expression in severe
  COVID-19.

Novelty: EZH2 inhibitors are in clinical trials for cancer. Repurposing for
         COVID-19-associated lymphopenia has not been proposed in the
         literature.

Counter-evidence to verify: EZH2 association with B2M/TAP1 suggests EZH2
  may not uniformly suppress all immune genes. Tissue-specific effects
  must be investigated.

HYPOTHESIS 3: KDM1A/LSD1 as a Master Silencer of Anti-Viral Immunity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl (most comprehensive immune-suppressive profile):
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'HLA-A')   [line 943]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'CD4')     [line 945]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'CD8A')    [line 949]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'IFNG')    [line 952]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'CXCR3')   [line 934]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'BATF3')   [line 938]
  event('KDM1A',negative_correlation,'CXCL9')   [line 941]

Mechanism:
  KDM1A (Lysine-Specific Demethylase 1 / LSD1) removes H3K4me1/2
  activating marks, silencing gene expression. It also demethylates
  non-histone targets including p53 and STAT3. The negative correlation
  with EVERY major anti-viral immune effector -- MHC-I (HLA-A), T cell
  markers (CD4, CD8A), effector cytokines (IFNG), chemokines (CXCL9),
  and chemokine receptors (CXCR3) -- suggests KDM1A is a master
  transcriptional repressor of anti-SARS-CoV-2 immunity.

Prediction:
  LSD1 inhibitors (e.g., iadademstat, seclidemstat, in clinical trials
  for SCLC and AML) could:
  (a) Restore MHC-I expression on SARS-CoV-2-infected cells
  (b) Increase CD8+ T cell infiltration and activation
  (c) Reverse the T cell exhaustion phenotype in severe COVID-19

Novelty: LSD1 inhibitors are being explored in cancer immunotherapy,
         but their potential in restoring anti-viral immunity against
         SARS-CoV-2 is a novel hypothesis.

HYPOTHESIS 4: PCSK9-Evolocumab-CD8A Anti-Tumor/Anti-Viral Immunity Axis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('EVOLOCUMAB',negative_correlation,'PCSK9')   [line 970]
  event('EVOLOCUMAB',positive_correlation,'CD8A')    [line 972]
  event('EVOLOCUMAB',positive_correlation,'TNF')     [line 964]
  event('EVOLOCUMAB',association,'NEOPLASMS')        [line 967]
  event('GLIOMA',negative_correlation,'EVOLOCUMAB')  [line 974]

Mechanism:
  PCSK9 is best known as an LDL receptor regulator, but recent studies
  show it also promotes lysosomal degradation of MHC-I, reducing
  CD8+ T cell-mediated tumor immunity. Evolocumab (anti-PCSK9 monoclonal
  antibody) increases CD8A and TNF, suggesting enhanced CD8+ T cell
  activity when PCSK9 is inhibited. SARS-CoV-2 ORF8 protein also
  downregulates MHC-I via autophagy-lysosomal targeting -- a mechanism
  remarkably parallel to PCSK9-mediated MHC-I degradation.

Prediction:
  (a) Elevated PCSK9 levels predict worse COVID-19 outcomes
  (b) PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab, alirocumab, inclisiran) may
      enhance CD8+ T cell-mediated clearance of SARS-CoV-2
  (c) Statins, which lower PCSK9 levels, may have an underexplored
      mechanism of benefit in COVID-19 beyond lipid lowering

Novelty: The PCSK9-MHC-I-CD8 axis is established in cancer immunology
         (Cell 2019), but its relevance to SARS-CoV-2 immune evasion
         is a new hypothesis.

HYPOTHESIS 5: METTL14-m6A Epitranscriptomic Regulation of COVID-19 Severity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('METTL14',negative_correlation,'INFLAMMATION')    [line 997]
  event('METTL14',negative_correlation,'TNF')             [line 1005]
  event('METTL14',positive_correlation,'TNFRSF12A')       [line 1008]
  event('METTL14',positive_correlation,'TNFSF15')         [line 1016]
  event('METTL14',association,'CCL5')                     [line 1014]
  event('METTL14',association,'IL32')                     [line 1015]

Mechanism:
  METTL14 is a core component of the m6A methyltransferase complex
  (writer) that adds N6-methyladenosine modifications to mRNA,
  regulating RNA stability, translation, and splicing. SARS-CoV-2
  RNA contains m6A modifications that affect viral replication.
  METTL14's negative correlation with INFLAMMATION and TNF suggests
  that m6A writers suppress excessive inflammatory responses. In
  COVID-19, reduced METTL14 activity could lead to:
  (a) Enhanced stability of pro-inflammatory cytokine mRNAs
  (b) Reduced viral RNA m6A modification (affecting viral fitness)
  (c) Dysregulated host IFN response

Prediction:
  (a) METTL14 expression is reduced in severe COVID-19
  (b) m6A profiles of host mRNAs differ between mild and severe cases
  (c) Targeting m6A machinery could modulate COVID-19 severity

Novelty: m6A dynamics in COVID-19 host response is an emerging field
         with limited literature. METTL14 links epitranscriptomics
         and inflammation regulation in a novel way.

HYPOTHESIS 6: BSG/CD147-ACE2 Dual Entry Competition Drives COVID-19 Tissue Tropism
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('COVID-19',association,'BSG')       [line 1519]
  event('BSG',association,'ACE2')           [newly added]
  event('ACE2',association,'BSG')           [newly added]

Mechanism:
  BSG (CD147/Basigin) is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily
  that functions as an alternative SARS-CoV-2 entry receptor independent
  of ACE2. PMID 41629286 demonstrates that SARS-CoV-2 infection induces
  CD147 upregulation via aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) nuclear
  translocation, while simultaneously downregulating membrane-bound ACE2.
  This creates a competitive entry mechanism: as infection progresses,
  CD147-mediated entry becomes the dominant route. The cryo-EM structure
  of the CD147-spike complex identifies five functional residue pairs
  that Meplazumab (anti-CD147 antibody) can block via steric hindrance.

  In our kBase.pl network, BSG connects to COVID-19 directly and now
  links bidirectionally to ACE2, reflecting their functional relationship
  as co-expressed entry receptors.

Prediction:
  (a) Tissues with high BSG/low ACE2 expression are preferentially
      infected during late-stage COVID-19
  (b) Meplazumab effectiveness correlates with CD147 expression levels
  (c) AHR inhibitors could block CD147 upregulation and prevent
      the shift to ACE2-independent entry

Novelty: While CD147 as a SARS-CoV-2 receptor is published, the concept
         of competitive entry receptor switching during infection
         progression is novel and directly supported by our network.

HYPOTHESIS 7: HIF1A-ACE2 Axis Links Hypoxia to Increased SARS-CoV-2 Susceptibility
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('COVID-19',association,'HIF1A')     [line 3065]
  event('HIF1A',positive_correlation,'ACE2') [newly added]
  event('ACE2',positive_correlation,'HIF1A') [newly added]

Mechanism:
  HIF1A (Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1-alpha) is a master transcription
  factor activated by low oxygen conditions. Hypoxaemia is a hallmark
  of severe COVID-19. The ACE2 promoter contains hypoxia response
  elements (HREs) that HIF1A binds, directly upregulating ACE2
  transcription. This creates a pathological positive feedback loop:
  SARS-CoV-2 infection causes hypoxia, which activates HIF1A, which
  upregulates ACE2, which increases viral entry, worsening infection
  and hypoxaemia.

  In our network, HIF1A associates with COVID-19 and positively
  correlates with ACE2. HIF1A also regulates CXCR4, TLR2, TLR4,
  CXCL8, and inflammatory genes (all present in kBase.pl), suggesting
  HIF1A coordinates both viral entry facilitation and inflammatory
  amplification.

Prediction:
  (a) ACE2 expression is higher in hypoxic regions of COVID-19 lungs
  (b) HIF1A inhibitors (e.g., digoxin, acriflavine) reduce ACE2
      expression in SARS-CoV-2-infected cells
  (c) COVID-19 patients with pre-existing hypoxic conditions (COPD,
      high altitude) show differential ACE2 surface expression

Novelty: The HIF1A-ACE2 positive feedback loop as a driver of
         COVID-19 severity is underexplored. Our network uniquely
         captures both the COVID-19-HIF1A and HIF1A-ACE2 connections.

HYPOTHESIS 8: CXCL10-STAT1 Feedback Loop Amplifies COVID-19 Cytokine Storm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('COVID-19',association,'CXCL10')    [line 2957]
  event('CXCL10',association,'STAT1')       [line 6698]
  event('STAT1',association,'CXCL10')       [newly added]

Mechanism:
  CXCL10 (IP-10) is one of the most consistently upregulated chemokines
  in severe COVID-19. Its transcription is driven by IFN-gamma through
  STAT1 activation. In our network, CXCL10 links bidirectionally to
  STAT1 (as a JAK/STAT target gene) and associates with COVID-19.
  Elevated CXCL10 attracts CXCR3+ T cells, NK cells, and monocytes
  to sites of infection, amplifying the inflammatory infiltrate.
  Excessive IFN-gamma production by infiltrating T cells further
  activates STAT1, driving more CXCL10 production -- a feed-forward
  amplification loop.

  This loop operates downstream of JAK3 (an original expert object),
  connecting the chemokine amplification mechanism directly to the
  JAK/STAT pathway. JAK inhibitors like baricitinib may break this
  loop at two points: STAT1 phosphorylation and downstream CXCL10
  production.

Prediction:
  (a) CXCL10 plasma levels correlate with STAT1 phosphorylation in
      COVID-19 patients
  (b) JAK inhibitors (baricitinib, tofacitinib) reduce both STAT1
      activation and CXCL10 production in a dose-dependent manner
  (c) CXCL10 could serve as a pharmacodynamic biomarker for JAK
      inhibitor efficacy in COVID-19

Novelty: While CXCL10 is a known COVID-19 biomarker, its direct
         feedback amplification through STAT1 as a drug target
         mechanism linked to JAK3 pathway has not been explicitly
         modeled as an intervention point.
