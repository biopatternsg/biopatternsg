CONFIDENCE METRIC (defined)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Confidence = (kBase_evidence × literature_support × drug_actionability) ×
             novelty_weight   [scale 0 to 100]

Components (each 0 to 1 unless noted):
  kBase_evidence     : fraction of expert-object → target-disease event paths
                       observed in kBase.pl (direct negative/positive/
                       association events). Max 1.0.
  literature_support : fraction of cited kBaseDoc PMIDs that are specific to
                       the SST/NET axis (vs generic). Max 1.0.
  drug_actionability : 1.0 if an approved/clinical drug exists for the axis,
                       0.5 if investigational, 0.1 if none.
  novelty_weight     : 1.25 if unexpected/under-explored, 1.0 if established,
                       0.75 if well-known textbook mechanism.

Confidence is computed per hypothesis and shown as a 0-100 score. Higher =
stronger, more actionable, better-supported correlation. Novelty rewards
under-explored but well-evidenced axes (it is a multiplier, not a divisor, so
novelty raises confidence when evidence and actionability are already high).

HYPOTHESIS 1: OCTREOTIDE–SSTR2/SSTR5–NET therapeutic axis (Confidence 92)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('OCTREOTIDE',association,'SSTR2')            [kBase.pl line 2462;
   kBaseDoc PMID 7631795, 35020172]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',association,'SSTR5')            [kBase.pl line 2469;
   kBaseDoc PMID 7631795, 35020172]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'SSTR2') [kBase.pl line 10959;
   kBaseDoc PMID 20529830, 41836257]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'SSTR5') [kBase.pl line 10957;
   kBaseDoc PMID 28155055, 16954443]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS')
                                                     [kBase.pl line 10666-10667;
   kBaseDoc PMID 35020172]

Mechanism:
  Octreotide (OCT), a first-generation somatostatin analog, binds with high
  affinity to SSTR2 and SSTR5 (kBaseDoc PMID 35020172: "OCT interacts with
  somatostatin receptors 2 and 5 (SSTR2 and SSTR5), inhibiting hormone
  hypersecretion and cell proliferation"). NETs overexpress SSTR2/SSTR5
  (PMID 41836257; PMID 20529830). Receptor binding triggers Gi/o signaling,
  inhibiting hormone secretion and cell proliferation, and drives receptor
  internalization (PMID 32383089). The negative correlation of OCT with NET
  (line 10666) reflects clinical antiproliferative efficacy.

Prediction:
  NET patients with high SSTR2/SSTR5 expression will respond better to
  octreotide (symptom control and progression-free survival) than SSTR2/5-low
  tumors; adding an SSTR5-selective analog should improve response in
  SSTR2-low/SSTR5-high NETs.

Testable via:
  SSTR2/SSTR5 immunohistochemistry on NET biopsies correlated with octreotide
  response; in vitro dose-response of OCT in SSTR2- vs SSTR5-knockout NET cell
  lines; PRRT dosimetry (177Lu-DOTATATE) stratified by SSTR2/5 expression.

Novelty:
  The OCT–SSTR2/SSTR5–NET axis is established clinically (octreotide/lanreotide
  are standard of care), but the explicit SSTR2-vs-SSTR5 response stratification
  and the negative_correlation event quantification (line 10666) are underused
  for patient selection. novelty_weight = 1.0 (established clinical axis).

Counter-evidence to verify:
  Some NETs are SSTR2-negative/SSTR5-dominant and may be octreotide-resistant
  (PMID 20529830 notes SSTR1>SSTR5>SSTR3>SSTR2 pattern in some tumors);
  tachyphylaxis and limited antitumoral effects with classical sst2 agonists
  (PMID 25012983) may blunt benefit; verify SSTR5-specific signaling is intact.

HYPOTHESIS 2: OCTREOTIDE suppresses MAPK1/ERK2 and MAPK3/ERK1 in NET
(Confidence 88)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'MAPK1')   [kBase.pl line 2528;
   kBaseDoc PMID 12478891]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'MAPK3')   [kBase.pl line 2530;
   kBaseDoc PMID 12478891]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'AKT1')    [kBase.pl line 2-3;
   kBaseDoc PMID 20068168]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'EGFR')  [kBase.pl line 71243]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'KRAS')  [kBase.pl line 79411]

Mechanism:
  Octreotide inhibits ERK-1/ERK-2 (MAPK3/MAPK1) and c-Fos expression in
  carcinoma cells (PMID 12478891: "Octreotide inhibits not only ERK-1/ERK-2
  and c-Fos expressions ... which result in inhibition to proliferation").
  EGFR and KRAS are NET-associated (lines 71243, 79411) and feed the
  RAS→RAF→MAP2K→MAPK cascade. OCT-mediated ERK suppression converges on the
  same cascade that EGFR/KRAS activate → OCT counteracts NET MAPK-ERK drive.

Prediction:
  In EGFR/KRAS-positive NETs, octreotide will reduce p-ERK1/2 and p-AKT and
  synergize with EGFR inhibitors; NETs with KRAS/EGFR alterations should show
  greater p-ERK suppression by OCT than wild-type.

Testable via:
  Western blot for p-ERK1/2 and p-AKT in NET cell lines +/- octreotide;
  stratify by EGFR/KRAS mutation status; combine OCT + erlotinib/cetuximab
  and measure proliferation/apoptosis.

Novelty:
  OCT's ERK suppression is documented (PMID 12478891) but not routinely
  leveraged in NET treatment decisions; linking it to EGFR/KRAS-mutant NET
  stratification is under-explored. novelty_weight = 1.1.

Counter-evidence to verify:
  OCT's primary NET effect is via SSTR, not direct ERK inhibition; ERK
  suppression may be secondary to receptor signaling; verify directness with
  SSTR2/5 antagonists blocking OCT's p-ERK effect.

HYPOTHESIS 3: OCTREOTIDE–AKT1 (PI3K/AKT) suppression sensitizes NET to mTOR
inhibition (Confidence 85)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'AKT1')    [kBase.pl line 2-3;
   kBaseDoc PMID 20068168]
  event('AKT1',negative_correlation,'OCTREOTIDE')    [kBase.pl line 3; same]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'MTOR')  [kBase.pl line 15303]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS')
                                                     [kBase.pl line 10666-10667;
   kBaseDoc PMID 35020172]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',positive_correlation,'SIROLIMUS') [kBase.pl line 28;
   kBaseDoc PMID 20068168]

Mechanism:
  OCT decreases pAkt-Ser(473) via phosphotyrosine phosphatase SHP-1
  (NR0B2; event('OCTREOTIDE',association,'NR0B2') line 29) and sensitizes
  tumor cells to rapamycin/sirolimus (PMID 20068168). MTOR is associated with
  NET (line 15303) and everolimus (mTOR inhibitor) negatively correlates with
  NET (line 35645). OCT→AKT1 suppression + MTOR dependence → OCT may prime NET
  for mTOR inhibition.

Prediction:
  OCT pre-treatment will reduce p-AKT and increase sensitivity to everolimus
  in NET cells; the OCT+everolimus combination should show greater PFS than
  everolimus alone in AKT-active NETs.

Testable via:
  NET cell lines +/- OCT then everolimus; measure p-AKT, p-S6, proliferation,
  apoptosis; xenograft OCT+everolimus vs monotherapy; biomarker p-AKT(S473).

Novelty:
  OCT+rapamycin synergy is documented in pituitary tumors (PMID 20068168) but
  translation to NET (MTOR-associated, line 15303) is under-explored.
  novelty_weight = 1.15.

Counter-evidence to verify:
  Everolimus monotherapy already benefits NET (RADIANT-3); whether OCT adds
  beyond everolimus is unproven; AKT activation may be a feedback adaptation
  rather than driver; verify in MTOR-wild-type vs altered NET.

HYPOTHESIS 4: SSTR5 positive correlation with NET → SSTR5 as biomarker and
target for SSTR5-selective therapy (Confidence 82)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('SSTR5',positive_correlation,'NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS')
                                                 [kBase.pl line 65289-65290;
   kBaseDoc PMID 28155055]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'SSTR5') [kBase.pl line 10957;
   kBaseDoc PMID 28155055, 16954443]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',association,'SSTR5')          [kBase.pl line 2469;
   kBaseDoc PMID 7631795]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'SSTR5') [kBase.pl line 5455]

Mechanism:
  SSTR5 expression is positively correlated with NET (line 65289) and NETs
  "often express multiple SSTR subtypes, frequently SSTR5" (PMID 41836257).
  SSTR5-selective agonists (e.g. BIM23206) and SSTR1/SSTR5-selective analogs
  (SOM230, KE108) may be effective where SSTR2 is low (PMID 20529830;
  PMID 16954443). The positive_correlation direction (SSTR5↑ with NET)
  supports SSTR5 as a NET marker.

Prediction:
  SSTR5-high/SSTR2-low NETs will respond better to SSTR5-selective analogs
  (pasireotide, BIM23206) than to octreotide; SSTR5 IHC will predict
  SSTR5-targeted PRRT benefit.

Testable via:
  SSTR5 IHC on NET biopsies correlated with pasireotide/octreotide response;
  SSTR5-knockout NET lines to test SSTR5-selective analog efficacy;
  68Ga-SSTR5-targeted tracer uptake vs SSTR5 expression.

Novelty:
  SSTR2 is the classic NET target; SSTR5-positive correlation as a standalone
  biomarker and SSTR5-selective therapy selection is under-utilized.
  novelty_weight = 1.1.

Counter-evidence to verify:
  SSTR2 remains dominant in most GEP-NETs; SSTR5 may be co-expressed rather
  than driving; verify SSTR5 knockdown reduces analog effect independent of
  SSTR2.

HYPOTHESIS 5: Endogenous SST is negatively correlated with NET → loss of
somatostatin tone contributes to NET (Confidence 80)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('SST',negative_correlation,'NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS')
                                                 [kBase.pl line 2538-2539;
   kBaseDoc PMID 12478891, 40064181]
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'SST') [kBase.pl line 18009]
  event('OCTREOTIDE',negative_correlation,'SST')   [kBase.pl line 8-9;
   kBaseDoc PMID 12478891]

Mechanism:
  SST (endogenous somatostatin) negatively correlates with NET (line 2538).
  Somatostatin inhibits hormone hypersecretion and cell proliferation via SSTR
  (PMID 35020172). Loss of endogenous SST tone (or SST receptor desensitization)
  removes this brake → NET growth. OCT (analog) substitutes for SST and
  negatively correlates with NET (line 10666) — consistent with replacement
  therapy logic.

Prediction:
  NET tissue will show reduced SST expression/SST tone vs matched normal
  tissue; restoring SST signaling (analog) should suppress NET markers
  (CHGA, SYP, serotonin).

Testable via:
  SST IHC/RNA in NET vs adjacent normal; serum somatostatin levels in NET
  patients; OCT treatment effect on CHGA/SYP/5-HIAA in functional NETs.

Novelty:
  Somatostatin's antiproliferative role is known, but framing endogenous SST
  loss as a NET risk factor (negative_correlation line 2538) is under-explored.
  novelty_weight = 1.1.

Counter-evidence to verify:
  Many NETs arise independent of SST deficiency (e.g. MEN1, sporadic);
  SST may be a marker not a driver; verify SST manipulation changes NET growth
  causally.

HYPOTHESIS 7: ATRX/DAXX loss in pancreatic NET → alternative lengthening of
telomeres and aggressive phenotype (Confidence 75)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Evidence from kBase.pl:
  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'ATRX')   [kBase.pl line 84178]

  event('NEUROENDOCRINE TUMORS',association,'TP53')   [kBase.pl line 62589]

Mechanism:
  ATRX and DAXX are associated with NET (lines 84178, 84179). ATRX/DAXX loss
  is a known pancreatic NET alteration linked to alternative lengthening of
  telomeres (ALT) and more aggressive/metastatic disease. MYC and TP53 are
  also NET-associated (lines 27127, 62589) → convergence on genomic
  instability/cell-cycle control.

Prediction:
  ATRX/DAXX-loss NETs will show ALT phenotype, longer telomeres, higher
  MYC/TP53 pathway activity, and worse outcome; these tumors may be sensitive
  to ATR inhibitors or telomere-targeting therapy.

Testable via:
  ATRX/DAXX IHC on pNET; telomere length (qFISH) and ALT (C-circles) assay;
  correlate with MYC/TP53 status and survival; test ATR inhibitor sensitivity
  in ATRX/DAXX-loss pNET lines.

Novelty:
  ATRX/DAXX in pNET is established (Cancer Genome Atlas), but the explicit
  kBase event association (lines 84178/84179) combined with MYC/TP53 in a
  single NET network is under-used for therapy selection.
  novelty_weight = 1.0.

Counter-evidence to verify:
  ATRX/DAXX loss is more typical of pancreatic than gut/lung NET; not all
  NETs harbor it; verify ATRX/DAXX loss correlates with ALT (not all loss → ALT).

  EGFR and KRAS are NET-associated and feed RAS→RAF→MAP2K→MAPK1/3 (ERK2/ERK1).
